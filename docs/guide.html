How Drift works

A timer you start in a second, from any app, and a plan for your day that starts itself. Here's everything it does.

The basics

Drift lives in two places: a small widget in the top-left corner of your screen, and an icon in the menu bar. There's no Dock icon and no window to keep track of. The first time you open it, a short welcome guide shows you around; reopen it any time from the menu bar icon → Getting Started.

The one thing to remember: press ⌥ Space from any app, write what you're working on and for how long, press Return.

Writing a timer

Press ⌥ Space. A box opens over whatever you're doing and asks What are you working on? Write the task, then how long. The time lights up in orange as you type, so you can see Drift understood, and a preview underneath shows the exact timer Return will start.

Under the box you'll find one-click lengths (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 45 minutes, 1 hour, 2 hours; or ⌘ 1–⌘ 9) and your recent tasks with their usual length. If you've written a task, a length chip starts that task for that long.

The widget

The widget shows one line: a dot (filled while running, hollow when paused), the task, the time left and a pause button. Drag it anywhere; it remembers its spot, even across displays, and it floats above full-screen apps.

  • Click the task to rename it right there. A timer without a name shows a faint + Add task.
  • Click the time to open the dial.
  • Click the arrow to expand it: every timer, ±5 minutes, today's plan, focus sound, and a button for a new timer.

Changing the time

Click any time to open the dial. Drag around the ring and the timer follows: a minute per notch, a full turn is an hour. You can also scroll, or use the arrow keys (hold ⇧ for five minutes). Buttons underneath add or remove 1 or 5 minutes, and presets set 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 or 45 minutes, 1 or 2 hours in one tap. Done keeps the change; Cancel puts the old time back.

Several timers

Start as many as you like. One stays in view; the button beside it shows how many others there are (+2). Expand the widget to see them all with their own pause, cancel, name and time. Click one to bring it to the top.

Planning your day

Write when instead of (or as well as) how long, and Drift adds a block to your plan instead of starting it now. The time of day and the days light up in blue.

Gym 6-7pm mon wed friMonday, Wednesday and Friday evenings
Email at 1pm for 30mOnce, today (or tomorrow if it's past 1)

Today's blocks appear in the expanded widget and the menu bar, marked Next, Now, Done or Skipped. A line under the timer tells you what's next.

When a block comes up

Drift plays a soft chime, shows a notification and puts a card on the widget: Start, In 5 min or Skip today. If you're heads down and ignore it, the block starts by itself after a minute. Its timer ends when the block ends, so starting ten minutes late keeps your time box instead of pushing your whole day.

Changing a block

Click any block to edit it. Drag it along its timeline to move it; drag its right edge to change the length; or nudge the start, pick a length and choose the days it repeats. For a repeating block that's on today, you choose: Just today, or every day it repeats. A block changed for one day is marked today. If the block is running, its timer follows the change.

Pomodoro

Type pomodoro (or press ⌘ P in the launcher) for 25 minutes of focus, then a 5 minute break, on repeat; every fourth break is 15 minutes. Expanded, the widget shows which round you're on and what's next. Use your own lengths with 50/10. Skip ahead any time.

When a timer ends

A three-note chime plays and the widget gently pulses until you look; by default it repeats every few seconds for up to two minutes, or once if you prefer. A notification says what finished ("Email is done"). Dismiss it, restart it, or add five more minutes.

Focus sounds

Optional, and off until you turn it on in the expanded widget or the menu bar. Every sound is generated live on your Mac, never streamed:

  • Nature: Rain, Ocean, Fireplace
  • Chill: Lo-fi, Ambient, Soft Pad
  • Focus: Hush (deep, even noise), Binaural Focus, Binaural Calm (best with headphones)

It plays while a timer runs, fades out when nothing's running, and goes quiet during breaks.

Voice

Press ⌥ Space twice (or ⌥ V, or the microphone in the launcher) and say it: "twenty five minutes for reading", "add five minutes", "pause". It stops listening when you stop talking. Voice is optional; macOS asks for permission the first time. Speech is recognised on your Mac and never stored.

Hiding it, and the menu bar

Press ⌥ T to hide the widget; the time moves next to the menu bar icon, and timers keep running. Press it again to bring the widget back. The menu bar icon is a small ring that empties as time passes. Its menu lists every timer (with pause, ±5 minutes, change time, rename, duplicate, cancel), today's plan, quick starts, Pomodoro, focus sound, Getting Started and Settings.

The three global shortcuts can be changed in Settings.

Settings

One short page: default length, quick lengths, how long finished timers stay, theme, widget size and opacity, the chime and notifications, focus sound, shortcuts and opening at login. Shortcuts, Siri and Raycast can also start timers through drift:// links.

Privacy

No account, no analytics, and no network access at all. Your timers, plan and settings stay on your Mac. Details on the privacy page; problems and fixes on the support page.
