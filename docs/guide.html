How Drift works

A timer you start in a second, from any app. Line up a few blocks to run back to back, or plan your day and let each one start itself. Here's everything it does.

The basics

Drift lives in two places: a small widget in the top-left corner of your screen, and an icon in the menu bar. There's no Dock icon and no window to keep track of. The first time you open it, a short welcome guide shows you around; reopen it any time from the menu bar icon → Help → Getting Started.

The one thing to remember: press ⌥ Space from any app, write what you're working on and for how long, press Return.

Writing a timer

Press ⌥ Space. A box opens over whatever you're doing and asks What are you working on? Write the task, then how long. The time lights up in orange as you type, so you can see Drift understood, and a preview underneath shows the exact timer Return will start.

Reply to emails 25m"Reply to emails", 25 minutes
Code review for an hour and a half"Code review", 1 hour 30 minutes
Draft proposalNo time? Drift offers your default length in grey. Press Tab or Return to take it.
25A bare number means minutes. Name it later.
1:30, 90s, 2h 15mAny way you'd write a time
inbox 10m, plan the day 10mA session: time blocks back to back (see Sessions)
pomodoro, 50/10Focus and break, alternating (see Pomodoro)
add 5 minutes, pause, resume code review, cancel standup prepChange a timer that's already running

The box wraps onto more lines as you write, so a long list stays in view. While it's empty you'll see one-click lengths (5, 10, 15, 25, 45 minutes, 1 hour; or ⌘ 1–⌘ 9 for your quick starts) and your recent tasks with their usual length. They step aside once you start typing. If you've written a task but no time, a length starts that task for that long. To close the launcher, press Esc, click outside it or click ×.

The widget

With nothing running, the widget asks What are you working on? Click the question to open the launcher, or start right there: ▶ Start 25m (your default length), one tap on 10m, 15m, 45m or 1h, or ⋯ to pick any length on the dial.

While a timer runs, the widget shows one line: a dot (filled while running, hollow when paused), the task, the time left and a pause button. Drag it anywhere; it remembers its spot, even across displays, and it floats above full-screen apps.

  • Click the task to rename it right there. A timer without a name shows a faint + Add task.
  • Click the time to open the dial.
  • Click the arrow to expand it: every timer, the time blocks of a session, ±5 minutes, today's plan, focus sound, and a button for a new timer.

The widget, launcher and menu show only what fits the moment, and anything you've switched off in Settings → Features doesn't appear at all.

Changing the time

Click any time to open the dial. Drag around the ring and the timer follows: a minute per notch, a full turn is an hour. You can also scroll, or use the arrow keys (hold ⇧ for five minutes). Buttons underneath add or remove 1 or 5 minutes, and presets set 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 or 45 minutes, 1 or 2 hours in one tap. Done keeps the change; Cancel puts the old time back.

Sessions

A session is one timer made of time blocks that run back to back: each block starts when the one before it ends. Write the blocks with commas, then, or one per line. Put a name and a colon first if you like.

Morning startup: inbox 10m, plan the day 10m, review calendar 5m"Morning startup", three time blocks, 25 minutes in all
Code review 30m, then reply to emails 15mTwo time blocks, no name needed
Morning startup: inbox 10m, plan the day 10m at 8am weekdaysThe same, scheduled every weekday at 8

As you type, the launcher lists each block with its clock time and says when the whole session ends. A Starts row offers Now and the next three half hours (for example 2:00 PM, 2:30 PM, 3:00 PM). One tap adds "starting at 2pm today" to what you wrote, so what you see is what runs; you can also just type at 3pm.

The widget shows the block you're on, with a thin bar for the whole session. Expanded, every block is listed with its clock time, and Next moves straight on if you finish early. When a block ends, a card says what's done and what's next:

✓ Inbox done · Next: Plan the day, 10m · starts in 10s   Start now · Pause

How long the hand-off waits is up to you: Settings → Next time block starts: right away, after 5, 10 or 30 seconds, or when you press Start. When the last block ends you get Session complete, with Run again.

Named sessions are saved. Run one again with one click from the launcher's recent list or the menu bar (More Ways to Start, which becomes Sessions and More once you have saved sessions), or type its name.

Extra time

Time blocks don't stretch by default, so a session ends when you planned it to. You can still shorten a block or skip to the next one. To allow +5 minutes in sessions, turn on Settings → Extra time in sessions.

A Grace period (Off, 5, 10 or 15 minutes) caps how much extra time any one timer or time block can get, so a 20-minute task stays close to 20 minutes. Once it's used up, +5m is greyed out.

Several timers

Start as many as you like. One stays in view; the button beside it shows how many others there are (+2). Expand the widget to see them all with their own pause, cancel, name and time. Click one to bring it to the top.

Scheduling

Write when instead of (or as well as) how long, and Drift schedules it instead of starting it now. The time of day and the days light up in blue. Sessions can be scheduled the same way.

Deep work 9-10:30 weekdaysEvery weekday, 9:00 to 10:30
Client call at 2pm for 30mOnce, today (or tomorrow if it's past 2)
Weekly planning 15m fri at 4pmEvery Friday at 4
Prep for standup tomorrow at 9:15amOnce, with your default length

When you press Return, a Scheduled card at the top of the widget confirms what, when, each block's time for a session, and when Drift will start it, for example: Tomorrow at 9:00 AM, Drift checks in and starts it for you. Press Got it, Edit to change it, or Undo. You can also use the menu bar → More Ways to Start → Schedule a Session…

Today's sessions appear in the expanded widget and the menu bar, marked Next, Now, Done or Skipped. A line under the timer tells you what's next.

When it's time

Drift plays a soft chime, shows a notification and puts a card on the widget: Start, In 5 min or Skip today. If you're heads down and ignore it, it starts by itself after a minute. Its timer ends when it's scheduled to end, so starting ten minutes late keeps your time box instead of pushing your whole day.

Changing a scheduled session

Click it in the widget or the menu bar. Drag it along its timeline to move it; drag its right edge to change the length; or nudge the start, pick a length and choose the days it repeats. For one that repeats, you choose: Just today, or every day it repeats. One changed for a single day is marked today. If it's running, its timer follows the change.

Pomodoro

Type pomodoro (or press ⌘ P in the launcher) for 25 minutes of focus, then a 5 minute break, on repeat; every fourth break is 15 minutes. Expanded, the widget shows which round you're on and what's next. Use your own lengths with 50/10. Skip ahead any time.

When time's up

A three-note chime plays and the widget shakes, so you notice even with the sound off. The shake stops the moment you touch the widget; turn it off in Settings → Shake the widget when time's up. The widget shows a card:

TIME'S UP · Reply to emails · 25 minutes complete   Done · +5m · Restart

Done clears it. A session ends with Session complete and Run again. The notification reads "Time's up: Reply to emails". The chime repeats every few seconds for up to two minutes, or once if you prefer.

Focus sounds

Optional, and off until you turn it on in the expanded widget or the menu bar. Every sound is generated live on your Mac, never streamed:

  • Nature: Rain, Ocean, Fireplace
  • Chill: Lo-fi, Ambient, Soft Pad
  • Focus: Hush (deep, even noise), Binaural Focus, Binaural Calm (best with headphones)

It plays while a timer runs, fades out when nothing's running, and goes quiet during breaks.

Voice

Press ⌥ Space twice (or ⌥ V, or the microphone in the launcher) and say it: "twenty five minutes to reply to emails", "add five minutes", "pause". Sessions work too: "Morning startup, ten minutes of inbox, then ten minutes to plan the day, starting at 8". It stops listening when you stop talking. Voice is optional; macOS asks for permission the first time. Speech is recognised on your Mac and never stored.

Hiding it, and the menu bar

Press ⌥ T to hide the widget; the time moves next to the menu bar icon, and timers keep running. Press it again to bring the widget back. The menu bar icon is a small ring that empties as time passes.

Its menu is short: your timers (each with pause, ±5 minutes, change time, rename, duplicate and cancel), today's plan, New Timer… with quick lengths (5m, 10m, 15m, 25m, 45m, 1h), More Ways to Start (saved sessions, Pomodoro, Voice Command… and Schedule a Session…), focus sound, Hide Widget, Help (Getting Started, Help & Support) and Settings.

The three global shortcuts can be changed in Settings.

Settings

One short page:

  • Features: switches for Plan my day, Focus sounds, Pomodoro and Voice. Turn one off and it disappears from the widget, menu bar and launcher. Nothing is deleted; turn it back on any time.
  • Timer: default length, quick starts, Shake the widget when time's up (on by default), how long finished timers stay.
  • Sessions and extra time: Next time block starts, Extra time in sessions, Grace period.
  • Appearance, Sound, Focus sound, Shortcuts and opening at login.

Shortcuts, Siri and Raycast can also start timers through drift:// links.

Privacy

No account, no analytics, and no network access at all. Your timers, sessions, plan and settings stay on your Mac. Details on the privacy page; problems and fixes on the support page.
